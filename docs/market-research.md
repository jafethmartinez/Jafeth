## How to read this document, and what it is worth

Two things about method, because they change how much weight you should put on each number.

**1. Every source below was reached through a search engine, not by opening the page.**
The research environment blocked direct page fetches (`discoverroatan.net`,
`cruisemapper.com`, `cruisetimetables.com`, `carnival.com`, `viator.com`,
`cruisecritic.com`, `roatantourismbureau.com` and every other external site were all
refused by the network policy). So each figure is what a search engine extracted from
the cited page on 12 September 2026. That is good enough to establish a band or a
pattern. It is **not** good enough to quote a competitor's exact price back at a guest,
and it is not good enough to build the Port Time Planner on. Before anything here
becomes a promise on the website, open the page yourself.

**2. Confidence is labelled on every claim.**

| Tag | Means |
|---|---|
| **[V]** | Verified — an explicit figure attributed to a named source, with URL and date |
| **[E]** | Estimate — my inference from the verified figures. Reasoning shown. Not fact. |
| **[U]** | Unverified — I looked and could not confirm it. Treat as an open question. |

Where a number would be dangerous to get wrong — in-port hours, price bands — I have
said plainly when I could not verify it rather than filling the gap with a guess.

## 1. The ships

### 1.1 The two terminals, and who docks where

Roatán has two cruise terminals about 5 km apart on the south shore, and which one a
guest arrives at is decided entirely by who owns their cruise line.

**Isla Tropicale** (Dixon Cove — the terminal Carnival renamed from Mahogany Bay).
Carnival Corporation's own private destination, so it serves Carnival Corp brands only:
Carnival Cruise Line, Princess, Holland America, Costa, P&O and Cunard. **[V]**
— <https://discoverroatan.net/isla-tropicale-cruise-ship-port/>,
<https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/roatan-island-port-29> (seen 2026-09-12)

- Renamed from Mahogany Bay to Isla Tropicale on **20 May 2026**, alongside a new
  48,000 sq ft pool area called Mangrove Bay with a kids' splash pad and swim-up bar. **[V]**
  — <https://www.carnival-news.com/2026/05/21/carnival-cruise-line-unveils-new-pool-area-and-brand-identity-for-roatan-destination-isla-tropicale> (seen 2026-09-12)
- 24 acres. Docks **two ships at once**, no tendering. **[V]**
  — <https://cruisefever.net/ultimate-guide-to-mahogany-bay-cruise-port-in-roatan-honduras/>,
  <https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/roatan-island-port-29> (seen 2026-09-12)
- Daily capacity is quoted inconsistently: **8,000 guests/day** by one source, **9,000
  passengers at a time** by another. **[V, conflicting]** Either way, plan for ~8,000+ on a
  two-ship day.
- Since opening in 2009: ~9 million visitors, ~USD 750 million economic impact, a USD 93
  million Carnival investment, 1,300+ local jobs. **[V]**
  — as above, and <https://www.caribjournal.com/2025/04/11/carnival-cruise-line-roatan-isla-tropicale/> (seen 2026-09-12)

**Port of Roatán** (Town Center, Coxen Hole). Everyone who is not Carnival Corp: Royal
Caribbean, Norwegian, MSC, Celebrity, Oceania, Regent, Virgin Voyages, Viking. **[V]**
— <https://discoverroatan.net/port-of-roatan-cruise-ship-schedule/> (seen 2026-09-12)

- Handles ~**580,000 passengers a year on ~170 ship calls**, and can take **three ships at
  once — one alongside, two at anchor**. **[V]**
  — <https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/roatan-island-port-29> (seen 2026-09-12)
- 580,000 ÷ 170 ≈ 3,400 passengers per call average. **[E]** That average is dragged down
  by small ships; the big Royal Caribbean calls are double it.

**Why this matters to you commercially.** Your `PICKUPS` array already splits these two
correctly. But the guest at Isla Tropicale and the guest at Coxen Hole are not the same
customer. Isla Tropicale is a manufactured resort with a beach, a chairlift, shops and a
pool — Carnival has spent $93m making it good enough that guests never leave. Coxen Hole
is a working town centre terminal with a taxi plaza; there is nothing to keep a guest
there. **Coxen Hole passengers are structurally more likely to leave the port than Isla
Tropicale passengers.** **[E]**

The competing product at Isla Tropicale is not another tour operator — it is an **$18
all-day chairlift pass** ($11 for ages 3–11) to Carnival's own beach. **[V]**
— <https://discoverroatan.net/isla-tropicale-cruise-ship-port/> (seen 2026-09-12)
That is who you are actually bidding against for a Carnival guest, and it is why your
$65 tour has to do something the port beach cannot.

**[V]** — <https://www.carnival.com/itinerary/7-day-western-caribbean-cruise/galveston/breeze/7-days/wec>,
<https://www.carnival.com/itinerary/7-day-western-caribbean-cruise/new-orleans/liberty/7-days/cwb>,
<https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruises/itinerary/7-night-western-caribbean-from-orlando-port-canaveral-on-wonder/WN07PCN-2759925483>,
<https://www.globaljourneys.com/trip/ncl-blmimi8>,
<https://roatantourismbureau.com/community-updates/cruise-ships-roatan> (all seen 2026-09-12)

**The pattern: 8 to 9 hours alongside, starting 07:00–10:00.** **[E]** from five data
points across four lines. Consistent enough to design around; not a guarantee for any
individual sailing, and cruise lines label their own times "tentative".

**But hours alongside is not the number you can sell against.** Guest-usable time is
shorter at both ends:

- the gangway is not open the moment the ship touches the dock;
- **all-aboard is before departure**, and Cruise Critic's own guidance is that being back
  at the pier an hour before all-aboard is "the minimum, not a target". **[V]**
  — <https://discoverroatan.net/faq/> (seen 2026-09-12)

So the honest arithmetic, and what I would build the Port Time Planner on:

> **Usable window = (arrival + 30 min) to (departure − 60 min)**

| Call | Alongside | Usable window | Usable hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08:00 – 17:00 | 9:00 | 08:30 – 16:00 | **7h 30m** |
| 08:30 – 16:30 | 8:00 | 09:00 – 15:30 | **6h 30m** |
| 10:00 – 18:00 | 8:00 | 10:30 – 17:00 | **6h 30m** |

**[E]** — the 30/60 minute buffers are my judgement, informed by the "an hour before
all-aboard is the minimum" guidance above. **Set these two buffer numbers yourselves.**
You know how long the walk out of each terminal gate actually takes.

The consequence is uncomfortable and you should look at it directly: your four
**360-minute** tours — Little French Key, Pigeon Cay, the yacht charter — need 6 hours
door to door. On a 6h 30m usable window that leaves **30 minutes of slack on the whole
day**. On an 8:00–17:00 Carnival call you have 90 minutes of slack, which is fine. On an
8:30–16:30 Royal Caribbean call it is very tight, and on any delayed arrival it is gone.

**Recommendation:** the Port Time Planner should not just say yes or no. It should say
*yes with 90 minutes spare*, *tight — 30 minutes spare*, or *no*. A guest who books a
6-hour tour into a 6h 30m window and makes it back sweating will not recommend you. One
who is told "this one is tight, here is the 3-hour version instead" will.

### 1.5 Ship time vs Roatán local time — the thing that will strand a guest

Honduras is Central Standard Time year-round and **never observes daylight saving**.
Cruise ships normally keep the time of their embarkation port. **[V]**
— <https://discoverroatan.net/cruise-ship-time-v-roatan-local-time/>,
<https://timeanddate.com/worldclock/honduras/roatan> (seen 2026-09-12)

So while the US is on DST (roughly mid-March to early November):

| Ship sailed from | Ship's clock vs Roatán local |
|---|---|
| Galveston, New Orleans, Tampa (Central) | **1 hour ahead** |
| Miami, Port Canaveral, Fort Lauderdale (Eastern) | **2 hours ahead** |

**[V]** for the rule and the 1-hour Central case, explicitly for 8 March 2026
— <https://discoverroatan.net/cruise-ship-time-v-roatan-local-time/> (seen 2026-09-12).
The 2-hour Eastern case is **[E]** — arithmetic on the same rule, and the same source
states Eastern ports run two hours ahead of Roatán during DST.

The source also warns that all Roatán excursions run on **local** time and that guests
must account for the difference. **[V]** — same page.

**This is the highest-severity, cheapest-to-fix risk on your website.** A guest off
Carnival Celebration from Miami whose all-aboard is 16:30 *ship time* must be back at the
gangway at **14:30 Roatán local**. If your Port Time Planner takes "16:30" at face value
you will hand them a two-hour error and they will miss the ship. Nobody survives that
review.

Fix: the Planner must ask **"what port did your ship sail from?"** or, more simply,
**"is that time on ship time or island time?"** — and then show both, in words, on the
confirmation. Every page should carry one line: *all our times are Roatán local time, and
your ship's clock may be 1–2 hours ahead.*

## 2. The passengers

### 2.1 The market

- **37.2 million cruise passengers globally in 2025** — an all-time high, per CLIA's
  2026 State of the Cruise Industry report published 14 April 2026. **[V]**
  — <https://cruising.org/resources/state-cruise-industry-report-2026>,
  <https://www.travelpulse.com/news/cruise/cruise-industry-reports-record-high-passenger-numbers-for-2025> (seen 2026-09-12)
- **Honduras took 1,804,153 international visitors in H1 2026, up 18.5% year on year**,
  of which **63–65% were same-day excursionists** (~1.14–1.17 million). **[V]**
  — <https://www.nomadlawyer.org/honduras-tourism-record-visitor-arrivals-growth-2026> (seen 2026-09-12)
  Same-day excursionists at national scale are overwhelmingly cruise passengers, and
  Roatán is where they land. **[E]**

The market is growing and it is growing in exactly your segment. You are not fighting for
a shrinking pie.

### 2.2 Who they are

- **Average cruise passenger age 46.5. 36% are under 40. Around a third of cruises are
  multigenerational trips.** **[V]**
  — <https://www.cruisemummy.co.uk/cruise-industry-statistics-facts/> citing CLIA (seen 2026-09-12)
- Nationality mix: **[U]** — I could not verify a Roatán-specific or Western-Caribbean
  specific breakdown. **[E]** It is dominantly US: every homeport feeding Roatán
  (Galveston, New Orleans, Tampa, Miami, Port Canaveral) is American, and your
  `PAYMENTS` list (Cash App, Venmo, Apple Pay) is already built for American guests —
  which is a correct instinct, not a limitation.

**Group composition.** A third of sailings being multigenerational, plus an average age
in the mid-40s, means your typical enquiry is not two people. It is **4 to 8: two adults,
kids, often grandparents**. **[E]** Your `group` fields ("Up to 8 guests", "Up to 12",
"Up to 14") fit that well. The commercial implication is that you should quote and think
in **party totals, not per-person prices** — a family of six deciding between your $65pp
tour and Carnival's $18 chairlift is comparing $390 against $108, and per-person framing
hides that from them until the worst possible moment.

### 2.3 Spend, and the ship-versus-independent split

- **85% of cruise passengers take at least one shore excursion**, average spend
  **$60–180 per person per port day**. **[V, but weakly sourced]**
  — <https://www.cruiseshiptracking.com/blog/cruise-shore-excursion-booking-guide> (seen 2026-09-12).
  This is a commercial blog, not an industry body. The band is plausible and matches
  Roatán pricing in §4, so I would plan against it — but do not quote it as fact.
- **Cruise-line excursions are priced 30–80% above comparable independent tours.** **[V,
  weakly sourced]** — same page. Corroborated only by Roatán operators' own marketing
  claiming guests "save 30–50%" versus the ship's excursion desk
  (<https://discoverroatan.net/> and <https://www.roatanfivestars.com/>, seen 2026-09-12),
  which is advertising, not evidence. **[E]** Treat the true gap as "meaningfully
  cheaper", not a specific percentage.

**The ship-versus-independent split: [U]. I could not find a credible public number and
I am not going to invent one.** What I did find, repeatedly, is the *mechanism* rather
than the share: the one thing the cruise line's own excursions offer that nobody else can
is that **if a ship's excursion runs late, the ship waits**. **[V]**
— <https://www.cruiseshiptracking.com/blog/cruise-shore-excursion-booking-guide>,
<https://spaceatravels.com/private-vs-cruiseline-shore-excursion-myth/> (seen 2026-09-12)

That is the whole competitive question in one sentence. **What drives a passenger
independent is price and the promise of a smaller, more personal group. What holds them
back is fear of being left behind.** Your entire site is already built on the second half
of that sentence. That is the right strategy, and §5 is about doing it harder.

### 2.4 How far ahead they book

- Cruise lines open their own excursion booking as early as **180 days** before sailing
  and close as late as **5 days** before. Disney: 75 days (90–120 for repeat guests). **[V]**
  — <https://www.cruisebooking.com/articles/first-time-cruisers/how-to-book-cruise-excursions-in-advance>,
  <https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2022/09/09/mailbag-when-do-shore-excursions-become-available-book-my-royal-caribbean-cruise> (seen 2026-09-12)
- Common advice to passengers: book **60–90 days** out; **4–6 months** for high-demand
  experiences. Some operators ask for **120 days**. **[V]** — same sources.

**[E]** For an independent Roatán operator taking bookings by WhatsApp, expect a
different shape: a cluster **2–8 weeks out** from people who researched the port, plus a
substantial **last-minute tail** — guests on board with ship wifi, the night before or
the morning of. I cannot verify this. But two things in your own setup point at it: you
take no deposit, and WhatsApp is instant. Both invite late booking.

Two consequences:

1. **The site has to load and work on bad ship wifi**, which the README already treats as
   a design constraint. Keep it that way. This is worth more than any feature.
2. **A same-day WhatsApp path needs to be obvious.** A guest deciding at 07:40 on the
   Lido deck is a real customer segment, and they will not fill in a form.

## 3. Booking channels

### 3.1 Viator / Tripadvisor

- **Commission 20–25% standard.** The "Accelerate" programme lets you volunteer a
  **higher** rate (30–35%+) for better placement in search. **[V]**
  — <https://www.sambahq.com/ota-supplier-guide/ota-commission-rates>,
  <https://otaplaybook.com/viator-commission-rate-what-operators-actually-pay/>,
  <https://automate.travel/blog/viator-extranet-guide-2026/> (seen 2026-09-12)
- **Tripadvisor Experiences bookings flow through Viator at Viator's rate.** A free
  Tripadvisor business listing gets you reviews and analytics but no booking capability. **[V]**
  — <https://www.ticketinghub.com/en-US/blog/viator-supplier-faqs> and the Tripadvisor
  support forum threads on Experiences commission (seen 2026-09-12)
- **Bókun** (Viator's own booking system, as the README notes): from **$49/month**,
  **1–1.5% on direct bookings, 0% on Viator bookings**. **[V]**
  — <https://www.bokun.io/tour-and-activity-booking-system-pricing>,
  <https://hamzaliaqat.com/blog/bokun-pricing-2026> (seen 2026-09-12)

**Worth it at your scale? Yes — this is your first listing.** Two specific reasons beyond
reach. First, your `tripadvisor: ""` field is empty, which means you have **no public
reviews anywhere**, and reviews are the currency of this market; a free Tripadvisor
listing fixes that at zero cost and zero commission. Second, Bókun at $49/month with
**0% on Viator bookings and 1–1.5% direct** means it can also serve as the availability
system the README says you will eventually need — solving the "don't sell fourteen seats
in a twelve-seat van" problem the README names, while the OTA commission is only paid on
business you would not otherwise have had.

The 20–25% is real money and you must price for it (§4.3), but it is paid only on
incremental bookings.

### 3.2 GetYourGuide

- **Commission 25–30%, defaulting to 30% for new suppliers.** Established operators
  negotiate to 25–28%; the volume at which that conversation becomes realistic is around
  **1,000 bookings a year**. **[V]**
  — <https://otaplaybook.com/getyourguide-commission-rates/>,
  <https://automate.travel/blog/getyourguide-supplier-portal-guide-2026/> (seen 2026-09-12)
- Payout monthly, on the 5th business day of the following month. Bi-weekly payout costs
  **+2% commission**. **[V]** — same sources.
- Requires a **registered business and liability insurance**. Excludes resellers, other
  OTAs, DMCs and unregistered private guides. Application is ~30 minutes. **[V]**
  — <https://www.sambahq.com/ota-supplier-guide/getyourguide-supplier>,
  <https://getyourguide.supply/> (seen 2026-09-12)

**Worth it at your scale? Second priority, after Viator.** 30% is steep, you will not
reach the 1,000-bookings threshold to negotiate it down soon, and monthly-in-arrears
payment is hard on a cash business. But listing is free and the marginal cost of a second
listing once your product copy exists is an afternoon. **The blocker to check first is
liability insurance** — if you do not carry it, this channel is closed to you, and see
§3.6 for why that matters far beyond GetYourGuide.

### 3.3 Shore Excursions Group, Shore Excursioneer, Venture Ashore

These are cruise-excursion resellers — the segment whose entire business is placing
arriving passengers into tours.

- **Shore Excursions Group** publishes its *affiliate and agent* commissions: **8%** to
  affiliate partners, **10%** via TravelBrands, **12%** via Signature Travel Network. **[V]**
  — <https://www.shoreexcursionsgroup.com/travel-affiliates>,
  <https://www.travelmarketreport.com/canada/cruises/articles/travelbrands-partners-with-shore-excursions-group-10-commission> (seen 2026-09-12)
  **What they pay a local operator — the net rate — is not published. [U]** Those are
  sell-side numbers and tell you nothing about your margin. You have to ask.
  They already sell Roatán Garifuna/East End tours, which overlaps your Cultural
  Getaways directly — <https://www.shoreexcursionsgroup.com/tour/punta-gorda-and-garifuna-east-end/caropuntagord> (seen 2026-09-12)
- **Shore Excursioneer** runs an open operator-registration page and explicitly pitches
  itself against cruise lines "taking more than 60% of markup commissions". **[V]**
  — <https://www.shoreexcursioneer.com/operators> (seen 2026-09-12)
- **Venture Ashore** vets operators "through direct relationships and industry
  referrals" — relationship-led, no open sign-up. **[V]**
  — <https://www.ventureashore.com/partners> (seen 2026-09-12)

**Worth it at your scale? Shore Excursioneer, yes — send one email.** It costs nothing,
it is the right audience, and an operator console exists for exactly you. Venture Ashore
needs a referral you do not yet have. Shore Excursions Group is worth one enquiry
purely to learn their net rate.

### 3.4 Cruise Critic roll calls — read this before you post anything

**This channel will ban you if you use it the obvious way.** Cruise Critic's rules, verified:

- They ask that you do not use the forums to promote cruise-related websites: "an ad is
  an ad". **[V]**
- **"One-Post Wonders"** — posts by brand-new members touting tour operators in ports of
  call — "will be removed without notification", and **"the company's name may be
  censored from the forums without notice"**. **[V]**
- Roll call shore excursions must be **paid directly to the tour operator**. No
  third-party arranging. Travel agents may not arrange shore excursions on roll calls.
  Anyone "filling a bus" or making money from sharing excursions "runs the risk of losing
  their membership". **[V]**
- They will **never** remove a negative post about a tour company at that company's
  request. **[V]**

— all from <https://boards.cruisecritic.com/guidelines/> (seen 2026-09-12)

**Worth it? Yes, but only indirectly, and you must never post as yourselves.** The
mechanism that works is a **passenger** on a roll call saying "we've got 6 seats with
Martinez, anyone want to join" — and the rules are written to permit exactly that while
banning you from doing it. A censored brand name would be a self-inflicted wound you
could not undo.

So: **ask happy guests to post.** A card in the van saying *"if you found us useful,
mention us on your ship's Cruise Critic roll call"* is compliant, free, and lands in front
of the highest-intent audience in the market — people who have already decided to book
independently and are looking for people to split a van with. Note that the
pay-direct-to-operator rule is a perfect fit for your cash-on-the-day model.

### 3.6 The channel gate nobody mentions until you apply: insurance

This belongs in this section because it **decides which channels are open to you at all.**

Cruise lines will only contract with or promote operators who demonstrate specific cover:
**contingent general liability** (third-party bodily injury and property damage to guests
in your care), **contingent auto liability** (excess over local compulsory motor
insurance, covering owned, hired and non-owned vehicles), and **contingent watercraft
liability** (owned, hired and non-owned motorised craft carrying passengers for hire).
The industry position is blunt: tour operator liability insurance "is not optional...
because cruise lines refuse to advertise companies that work without one". **[V]**
— <https://excursioninsurance.com/frequent-asked-questions/>,
<https://excursioninsurance.com/blog/why-you-need-tour-operator-liability-insurance-for-your-excursion> (seen 2026-09-12)

A cruise line also has a duty to conduct a reasonable background check on operators it
recommends and sells. **[V]**
— <https://www.lawfuel.com/are-cruise-lines-liable-for-off-ship-accidents-what-every-2026-traveler-needs-to-know/> (seen 2026-09-12)

Pigeon Cay, snorkelling, glass-bottom, mangrove, tiki, plus the yacht) and **vehicles on

requires liability insurance to list at all (§3.2). **What you currently carry: [U] — I
have no visibility into it. Establish this before anything else in this document.** It

### 4.3 Two pricing rules that fall out of the bands

**Rule 1 — price for the commission before you list.** A tour you sell at $65 direct nets
$65 in cash. The same tour at $65 on Viator at 25% nets **$48.75**; on GetYourGuide at
30%, **$45.50**. If $65 is already your floor, OTA bookings are unprofitable. Either set
your list price so it survives 30% (a $65 direct tour lists at **$85–90**) or accept that
OTAs are a marketing cost that buys reviews rather than margin. **Do not list first and
discover this second.** **[E]** — arithmetic on the verified rates in §3.

**Rule 2 — your placeholder ranges are too wide to sell.** "$25–150" and "$65–150" do not
let a guest decide anything, and a family of six multiplying an unclear number by six
will abandon. Publish **one price per person for a defined product**, with add-ons listed
separately. Where the price genuinely depends on the boat — the yacht — keep "Ask us".
That is what it is for.

## 5. Recommendations, ranked by impact

Ranked for three people with no ad budget. Each says what to do and why the evidence
supports it.

### 1. Fix the ship-time bug in the Port Time Planner. Today.

The highest-severity item in this document (§1.5). Honduras never changes its clocks;
ships keep embarkation-port time; during US DST that is **1 hour ahead for Galveston, New
Orleans and Tampa sailings and 2 hours ahead for Miami and Port Canaveral**. **[V]** A
Planner that takes a guest's stated all-aboard time at face value can be two hours wrong
in the dangerous direction.

Ask "which port did your ship leave from?", or at minimum "is that ship time or island
time?". Show both times back. Put one line on every page: *all times are Roatán local;
your ship's clock may be 1–2 hours ahead.* **Zero cost. Prevents the one failure you
cannot recover from.**

### 2. Stop promising "back on time" and start proving it.

Six competitors already make that promise, and two of them quantify it better than you do
— Roatan Five Stars at one hour, **Roatan Exclusive at 1.5 hours** before departure. **[V,
§4.1]** An unquantified promise next to a quantified one reads as the weaker claim.

Concretely:
- **Pick a number and publish it.** "Back at the terminal gate 90 minutes before your
  all-aboard, in Roatán local time" — and then honour it.
- **Change the Planner from yes/no to three states**: *comfortable (90+ min spare)*,
  *tight (under 45 min spare — here's a shorter option)*, *no*. Your four 360-minute tours
  fit a 6h 30m usable window with only 30 minutes of slack (§1.4). Saying so builds more
  trust than saying yes.
- **Show the arithmetic.** "Your ship: 08:00–17:00. Gangway and walk out: 30 min.
  Back by: 16:00 island time. This tour: 6 hours. Spare: 90 minutes." Nobody else shows
  their working. That is a differentiator no competitor can copy without also showing
  theirs.

### 3. Settle the insurance question before anything else commercial.

Liability, auto and watercraft cover gates GetYourGuide listing, cruise-line and reseller
relationships, and every hotel referral conversation in `partner-outreach.md`. **[V,
§3.6]** A competitor is already advertising "licensed & insured" as a selling point. Your
own status is **[U]** to me. Find out what you carry, and if the answer is "not much",
this is the highest-return money you will spend — it is not overhead, it is market access.

- **10:00 arrivals** cannot take a 6-hour tour safely. Do not offer one.

### 8. Say the three things on the site that nobody else says.

  multigenerational family groups **[V, §2.2]**; a family business is the product they
  want, and it is the one thing no reseller can claim. Your `about.html` already has the
  team. Put them on the home page.
- **Quote party totals, not just per-person prices.** A family of six comparing your
  $65pp tour with Carnival's $18 chairlift is really comparing **$390 against $108**

  One caveat: with no deposit, **no-shows cost you the whole slot** on high-value tours.
